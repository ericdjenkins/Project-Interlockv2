Evidence & Research

The opportunity gap is measurable—and actionable.

9%

Black representation in STEM

Pew Research Center found that Black workers were 9% of the STEM workforce but 11% of all employed U.S. adults. Representation was lower in engineering and architecture (5%) and in life and physical sciences (6%).

Read the 2021 Pew report ↗
8%

Hispanic representation in STEM

In Pew’s analysis, Hispanic workers represented 8% of STEM workers while making up 17% of employment across all occupations—evidence of a substantial representation gap.

Review Pew’s findings ↗
8% / 15%

Current federal benchmark

The National Science Board reported that, in 2021, Black workers were 8% of STEM occupations versus 11% of the total workforce; Hispanic workers were 15% versus 18%. Its broader STEM definition differs from Pew’s.

See the National Science Board data ↗
8.1%

Growing demand for STEM talent

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects STEM employment to grow 8.1% from 2024 to 2034, compared with 3.1% for all occupations. The 2024 median STEM wage was $103,580.

View BLS projections ↗
87%

Encouragement matters

Pew found that 87% of Hispanic college-educated STEM workers reported at least one positive schooling experience involving career relevance, confidence, or encouragement to continue STEM classes.

Read the Pew analysis ↗
Tuition & fees

Massachusetts reduced a major barrier

MassEducate and MassReconnect make public community-college tuition and fees free for eligible Massachusetts residents, making advising, persistence, mentoring, and career connections even more important.

Official Massachusetts program details ↗

Methodology note: STEM estimates vary by occupational definition. Pew’s 2021 report uses a narrower set of STEM jobs; National Science Board figures use a broader federal STEM workforce definition. Project Interlock presents each figure with its source rather than treating them as directly interchangeable.

Greater Boston launch

Local depth. Scalable impact.

Our first phase focuses on communities within roughly 20 miles of Boston.

We will partner with Massachusetts community colleges—where eligible residents can receive tuition-and-fee support through MassEducate and MassReconnect—and public universities with engineering, computer science, data, and technology programs.
